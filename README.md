# Too Good To Go: Digital Platform & Data-Driven Operations Analysis

## Overview

Academic research project developed for the **Business Administration and New Technologies** course at the **University of West Attica**.

This project examines the operational mechanics of Too Good To Go's digital food redistribution platform, focusing on how digital systems and data flows support real-time inventory management, operational coordination, and data-driven decision-making.

## Project Highlights

- Analyzed the operational structure of Too Good To Go's digital food redistribution platform.
- Examined real-time inventory management and operational data flows.
- Explored how digital systems and business intelligence support data-driven decision-making.
- Identified AI-based forecasting as a potential future enhancement for operational optimization.
  
## Project Focus

The analysis explores:

- Digital platform operations
- Real-time inventory management
- Operational data flows
- Cloud-based synchronization
- Supply chain processes
- Business intelligence
- Data-driven decision-making
- Digital transformation

## Operational & Data Perspective

The project examines the end-to-end flow of surplus inventory through the platform, from merchant inventory assessment and digital availability updates to customer transactions and final collection.

It also explores how real-time data processing and centralized platform infrastructure can support inventory visibility, operational efficiency, resource optimization, and managerial decision-making.

## Key Workflow

1. Surplus inventory assessment and Surprise Bag creation
2. Real-time platform and inventory update
3. Customer discovery and digital transaction
4. Automated inventory adjustment
5. Local collection and transaction completion

## AI & Future Development

The research identifies **AI-based forecasting and automated data-driven forecasting** as potential future enhancements for improving surplus inventory prediction and operational optimization.

> AI and machine learning models were explored as future opportunities and were not implemented as part of this academic project.

## Scope & Limitations

This project is an academic business and data-oriented analysis. No machine learning models, production data pipelines, or MLOps systems were implemented. AI-based forecasting was explored as a potential future enhancement rather than implemented functionality.
